You can finish this course without finding a metric you trust in every
setting. You should leave knowing how to say what a measurement supports,
and where that support ends.

This is a course closing, not another technical lecture. There is no new
exercise or quiz. The presentations and defence remain in the
[Week 12 session](/sessions/12-what-you-would-report-instead/).

## Look back at the first number

In [Week 1](/lectures/week-01/), the bench offered a smaller score and a
tempting conclusion. Across the semester, the same candidates made that
conclusion harder to defend. More samples did not repair an insensitive
representation. Matching moments did not establish originality. Changing
the instrument could change the ranking without changing the recordings.

If you now hesitate before making a results-table entry bold, that hesitation
has a purpose. You know which conditions to ask for. Your measurement log
records how you learned to ask, including the predictions that did not survive
the experiment.

## Before you submit

Your final report does not need to prove that one metric answers every
question. It needs to make clear what you tested, what the evidence supports,
and what remains uncertain. A limitation you can explain is part of the
account, not something to hide behind a favourable score.

The [final report brief](/assessments/final-report/) remains the source of
requirements. Its [optional report template](/assessments/final-report/#report-template)
is there if it helps you organise that account. This closing adds no
submission requirement or deadline.

Thank you for bringing your questions to the bench and for staying with the
comparisons that did not turn out as expected. Finishing twelve weeks of
careful measurement is an achievement. Good luck with the final report.

The number can stay in the table. The conclusion is yours to defend.
