A lower FID is evidence only when the comparison survives scrutiny of the
measurement protocol. Helen Sandoval makes that question the centre of her
research and of SLOP8412.

## Research and background

Helen holds a continuing academic appointment in Slop University's School of
Continuous Improvement. Her background is in computational statistics; her
work moved from estimating distributional distances to asking when those
estimates support a model ranking.

She leads the Measurement Reliability Group, an internal research group that
studies how sampling and implementation choices change the conclusions drawn
from generative-model benchmarks. Her current interests include finite-sample
bias and uncertainty in comparisons whose reported scores differ only slightly.
The group's question is practical: what evidence should accompany the number
before another researcher relies on it?

## Role in SLOP8412

Helen convenes the course and leads the formal lectures. The
[Week 2 derivation](/lectures/week-02/) establishes what the Gaussian distance
measures; [Week 5](/lectures/week-05/) asks what happens when its moments must
be estimated. She returns to that distinction in the
[final report](/assessments/final-report/): a proposed replacement metric must
face the same scrutiny as FID.

## Supervision interests

Potential research directions include uncertainty-aware benchmark reporting
and experiments that separate estimator behaviour from model differences.
A useful project question names a comparison, a suspected failure and an
experiment that could contradict the hypothesis. These are supervision
interests within the fictional course setting, not an advertised PhD opening.

For implementation questions and reproducible examples, see
[Tomasz Wierzba](/people/tomasz-wierzba/). Use the course forum for questions
whose answers would help the whole class; keep individual matters private.
