---

## What we want

A distance between two **distributions**, not between two samples.

Nothing pairs a generated image with a particular real one, so the quantity has
to be defined on the distributions themselves.

---

## The definition

Wasserstein-2, as an optimisation:

W₂²(P, Q) = inf 𝔼‖X − Y‖²

taken over every joint distribution of (X, Y) whose marginals are P and Q.

---

## Why that is hard

Π(P, Q) is the set of all such joint distributions. It is infinite dimensional.

There is no formula to evaluate. In general you solve a transport problem.

```notes
- a figure would earn its place here: the set of couplings, with two fixed
  marginals pinned to the axes
```

---

## The move

Take P and Q Gaussian, and restrict the coupling to be jointly Gaussian too.

The objective now depends on nothing but the two means and the cross-covariance
between them. The search collapses to one matrix.

```notes
- linger here; this is the only step that costs anything
- everything after it is algebra that would hold for any pair of distributions
- ask: what have we assumed about the features? nothing yet. week 3.
```

---

## The mean term

𝔼‖X − Y‖² splits into a mean part and a centred part.

The mean part is fixed by the marginals alone, so no coupling can improve it:

‖μ₁ − μ₂‖²

---

## The covariance term

The centred part touches the coupling only through the cross-covariance.
Optimising over that one matrix leaves:

Tr(Σ₁ + Σ₂ − 2(Σ₁Σ₂)<sup>½</sup>)

---

## The cross term, twice

(Σ₁Σ₂)<sup>½</sup> in the papers.

(Σ₁<sup>½</sup> Σ₂ Σ₁<sup>½</sup>)<sup>½</sup> in careful implementations.

Different matrices, equal trace. The second is symmetric, and that is the one a
numerical routine can be trusted with. Week 4 is where it matters.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> d² = ‖μ₁ − μ₂‖² + Tr(Σ₁ + Σ₂ − 2(Σ₁Σ₂)<sup>½</sup>)

---

## The bench: A

A = N(0.05·**1**, I) against R = N(0, I).

Same covariance, so the trace term is Tr(2I − 2I) = 0.

2048 × 0.05² = **5.12**

---

## The bench: B

B = N(0, 1.1·I) against R = N(0, I).

Same mean, so the first term is 0.

2048 × (2.1 − 2√1.1) ≈ **4.88**

B is the better candidate, by a little.
